You mark a level on XAUUSD before the London session. Price moves beyond it, and you feel ready to enter. But your written plan says to wait for the breakout candle to **close**. It is still open.

XAUUSD means gold quoted in US dollars. A **breakout** here means price moving beyond the level you marked. It does not mean the move will continue.

This is where trading discipline becomes practical. **What did you plan, what is true now, and what will you record afterward?**

> **Quick answer:** trading discipline means checking a decision against the rules you wrote before the pressure appeared, then reviewing what you actually did. A winning trade can break your plan. A losing trade can follow it. Neither result tells the whole story on its own.

## Write the plan before you need it

Your [trading plan](https://proplogai.com/glossary/trading-plan) is the set of conditions you meant to use. For this **fictional** XAUUSD London example, you have written:

- **Setup:** a breakout beyond the level you marked before the session.
- **Confirmation:** the breakout candle must close before you decide whether the setup is present. A candle is one price bar for your chosen time period.
- **Size:** use the position-size method already recorded in your own plan.
- **Personal stop condition:** if you feel rushed, check the whole setup again before another decision.

These are one trader's sample conditions, not rules you should copy. Your own setup may be different. If you use a prop-firm account, check the current official terms and dashboard for **your exact firm, program and stage**. Your personal plan cannot replace them. The [prop-firm rules guide](/blogs/prop-firm-rules-guide) shows how to read those terms; the [rulebook guide](/blogs/prop-firm-trading-rulebook) helps you write your own rules clearly.

## Check the decision in front of you

Now the price is beyond your marked level, but the candle has not closed. You may think, “If I wait, I will miss it.” That feeling is real. It is not proof that the trade is good or bad.

The useful check is smaller: **has the confirmation you wrote down happened yet?** In this example, the answer is **not yet**. If you enter now, you have changed your written process. If you wait for the check, you may still choose not to trade later. A completed check is not an approved trade or a prediction.

This comparison is [setup compliance](https://proplogai.com/glossary/setup-compliance): does the idea in front of you match the setup conditions you wrote earlier? It is also the point of [rule-based trading](https://proplogai.com/glossary/rule-based-trading): use conditions you can observe and review, without claiming that every rule produces a win.

## Record the choice and the result on different lines

Imagine two **separate fictional outcomes** from that decision moment:

1. You wait for the candle to close, then take a setup that fits your plan. It ends at **−$35 net**. Your plan check is **Yes**; the result is a loss.
2. You enter before the candle closes. It ends at **+$40 net**. Your plan check is **No**; the result is a profit.

Neither outcome proves that the market will behave that way next time. The amounts are example results, not suggested risk or targets. The first trade followed the written confirmation and lost. The second skipped it and won. If you record only dollars, you lose the part that tells you what you did.

A [trading journal](https://proplogai.com/glossary/trading-journal) can keep **plan**, **actual action**, **plan match** and **net result** as separate fields. The [journal guide](/blogs/prop-firm-trading-journal) covers the full record. A [trade review](https://proplogai.com/glossary/trade-review) means looking back at one or more records to see what repeated, not judging yourself from one green or red result.

## Use the review to prepare one clearer check

After several sessions, ask where your written process most often changes. Is the candle-close condition missing when you feel rushed? Does your size change after a win or loss? Do you keep looking for trades after your planned session ends? One note is a clue; a few repeated notes give you a better question to investigate. They do not prove why a trade won or lost.

If the change often comes after a missed move or a loss, read [trading psychology](/blogs/trading-psychology-prop-firm) for the feeling behind a decision. If it shows up as repeated extra entries, the [overtrading guide](/blogs/overtrading-prop-firm-challenges) helps you compare those entries with your plan. For a later review across many records, use the [weekly trading review](/blogs/weekly-trading-review-template). Keep those deeper jobs on their own pages.

You do not need to promise that you will “always be disciplined.” Choose one condition from **your** plan that you want to make easier to see next session. Write it where you will check it. If your plan calls for a pause or stop, define that condition before the pressure appears. Do not borrow another trader's fixed loss count or assume one prop firm's limit applies to yours.

PropLogAI lets you manually record trade details, notes, emotions and whether you followed your own rules. You can use those records to review your decisions. It does not read a live broker account for you, approve or block an entry, confirm a firm's official rule status, or promise a better trading result.

Trading discipline is not a label you earn from a profitable day. It is the repeatable habit of asking: **what was my plan, what did I do, and what can I check next time?**
