You are watching an XAUUSD breakout in the London trading session. XAUUSD is gold quoted in US dollars; a breakout means price has moved beyond a level you marked in your own plan. Your usual [trading journal](https://proplogai.com/glossary/trading-journal) has that setup and the amount you plan to risk. But this is a funded account. Have you checked the rules for **this exact account**?

That is the extra job of a funded-account journal: **write down which rule you checked, where you found it, and what you still need to confirm.** Your note helps you remember your decision. It does not replace the firm's current agreement or account dashboard.

This guide adds an account check to a journal you already keep. If you need the full plan → trade → review method, start with the [prop-firm trading journal guide](/blogs/prop-firm-trading-journal).

## Start with your exact account, not another trader's rule

Before the trade, make one small account card. You can keep it beside your normal journal entry:

<div class="weekly-review-template" aria-label="Funded-account rule-source card">
  <p><strong>Firm and program:</strong> the exact name on your account.</p>
  <p><strong>Stage and account start:</strong> challenge, verification, funded stage or another stage named by your firm; include the start or change date when it affects the rules.</p>
  <p><strong>Official rule source:</strong> the current agreement or firm page for that program, plus the date you checked it.</p>
  <p><strong>Rule-day clock:</strong> the firm's reset time and time zone. Your clock in India may show a different date or time.</p>
  <p><strong>Relevant check:</strong> which restriction matters for this planned trade? Write “not checked yet” if you have not confirmed it.</p>
</div>

Why record all that? Because even one firm can use different rules for different programs, stages or account dates. [FTMO's official trading objectives](https://ftmo.com/en/trading-objectives/) are one current example of that variation. **Its limits are not your rules unless you hold the matching FTMO program and account.** For any firm, read the terms attached to your own account. The [prop-firm rules guide](/blogs/prop-firm-rules-guide) explains how to turn those terms into a personal rule sheet.

You may need to check a [daily drawdown limit](https://proplogai.com/glossary/daily-drawdown-limit), meaning the firm's limit for a rule day, or an [overall drawdown limit](https://proplogai.com/glossary/overall-drawdown-limit), meaning its broader loss boundary. Your program might use a different formula or have other conditions. A [consistency rule](https://proplogai.com/glossary/consistency-rule), if your account has one, can depend on a best day or best trade; the exact method and consequence are set by that firm.

If a rule or dashboard number is unclear, leave it marked **not checked yet** and verify it with the firm's official source or support. Do not turn a guess in your journal into permission to trade.

## Add the check to one XAUUSD journal entry

Here is a **fictional** London-session breakout entry. It shows the difference between your plan, what happened, and what the firm still has to confirm:

<div class="weekly-review-template weekly-review-template--filled" aria-label="Fictional funded-account journal entry">
  <p><strong>Before:</strong> XAUUSD · London session · my written breakout setup · planned loss $50.</p>
  <p><strong>Account check:</strong> I opened the official terms for my program and the firm's account dashboard before entry. I wrote down the rule-day reset time and the source I checked. This is a fictional record of a check, not a claim that the trade complied.</p>
  <p><strong>After:</strong> net recorded result −$58, including costs and the difference between planned and actual execution. I wrote whether I followed my own [trading plan](https://proplogai.com/glossary/trading-plan) and why I entered.</p>
  <p><strong>Firm status:</strong> not decided from this journal. Check the official dashboard or ask the firm if the status is unclear.</p>
</div>

The $50 was a **plan**, not a promise. In this made-up entry, the actual net loss is $58: $8 more than planned. A fill at a different price and trading costs can change the final result. This example does not suggest a risk amount, trade size or entry for your account.

Your journal may show the result you entered, but the firm may use account balance, open-trade profit or loss, fees and its own reset clock when checking a limit. **Only the firm's current terms and account records can tell you how its rule applies.** A journal note cannot declare that you have breached a rule, stayed safe, or qualified for a payout.

## Review the check, not only the profit or loss

A winning trade can still break your own written setup. A losing trade can still follow it. The result alone cannot score your discipline.

After a few trades, ask yourself:

1. Did I check the correct program and stage before each trade?
2. Did I record the source and reset time, or did I assume they were unchanged?
3. Did I follow the entry and risk steps in my own plan?
4. Which answer was missing when I felt the urge to trade?
5. What one check will I make easier before my next session?

You do not need a fixed five-point score. Write a short, honest note. For example: “I took the breakout before the condition in my plan was complete. The trade won, but the entry was early.” That is more useful for a [trade review](https://proplogai.com/glossary/trade-review) than calling the trade good because it made money.

You can use [PropLogAI's free trading journal](https://proplogai.com/trading-journal) to manually record trade details, notes, emotions, whether you followed your own rules, and screenshots. Those records can help you look back at your decisions. They are **your entries**, not broker-verified fills or an automatic firm-rule verdict.

Keep the next step simple: add the account card, mark unknown answers honestly, and check the exact firm's current terms before relying on any rule number. Your journal helps you remember what you knew and did. The firm determines the formal account status.
