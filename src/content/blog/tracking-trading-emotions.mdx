You are watching XAUUSD during the London session. Price is near a level you marked. Your plan says to wait for the breakout candle to close, but you want to enter now. You feel rushed.

That feeling is worth writing down. It does not mean you have done anything wrong. The useful question is **what you wanted to do, and what you actually did beside your plan**.

> **Quick answer:** to track trading emotions, write a short feeling word, the urge you noticed, your planned check and your action at the same point in each trade. Add the dollar result later, in its own field. After you have several entries, review what you recorded without assuming the feeling caused a win, loss or rule break.

## Start with one moment you can remember

This is a **fictional** XAUUSD example. XAUUSD means gold priced in US dollars. A breakout means price moves beyond a level the trader marked. That move may continue or turn back. The example is about a journal habit, not a reason to trade.

Before the entry, your written check says: **wait until the candle closes beyond the level**. While the candle is still open, you notice this:

> **Feeling:** rushed. **Urge:** enter before the close. **Plan:** wait for the close.

Those three short lines are enough to capture the moment. You can use your own plain words. “Nervous,” “keen,” or “I don't want to miss it” may describe your experience better than a fixed list of labels.

[Emotion tracking](https://proplogai.com/glossary/emotion-tracking) means keeping a short record of what you felt and what you did, so you can return to the decision later. The glossary gives the short definition; here is how to make that record useful.

## Add what you did, then the result

Now imagine you entered while that candle was still open. Write **“entered early”** in the action field. Do not change your earlier plan in the journal to make the entry look planned.

After the trade closes, suppose the fictional net result is **+$18**, after the costs counted in this example. Add that in a separate result field:

> **Action:** entered before my planned close. **Plan followed?** No. **Net result:** +$18.

The $18 does not turn the early entry into a planned entry. If the result had been −$18, that loss would not prove the feeling caused it either. Your action and your outcome answer different questions.

You can also feel rushed and still wait for your check. In that case, record **“rushed; wanted to enter early; waited for the close.”** A feeling is not itself a broken rule. And a calm feeling does not prove that you followed every rule.

## Make the record quick enough to repeat

Choose a point you can use on similar trades: for example, just before a planned entry and again after the trade. Label the moments so you do not mix a feeling **before** entry with one you noticed **after** the result.

For this example, one complete entry could read:

> **XAUUSD · London · breakout**  
> **Before entry:** felt rushed; wanted to enter while the candle was open.  
> **Written check:** wait for the candle to close beyond my level.  
> **Action:** entered before the close; plan followed: no.  
> **After exit:** net result +$18; felt relieved.  
> **Review question:** does this early-entry action appear again on similar breakouts?

Your own record can be shorter. If the feeling word is unclear, add one sentence about the moment. You do not need to write a long story or choose from a universal number of tags. What matters is that you can tell **when** you felt something and **what** you did.

## Review several entries without guessing at a cause

One entry tells you what you wrote about one trade. It does not tell you that being rushed always leads to an early entry, or that early entries always lose.

When you have several records, compare trades with the **same setup and written check**. Ask: “How often did I write that I wanted to enter early? On those occasions, what action did I record?” Then look at any entry where you felt rushed **and waited**. That counterexample matters too.

Here are three **fictional** notes from the same kind of XAUUSD London breakout. Each plan said to wait for the candle close:

- **Trade A:** felt rushed, entered early, finished **+$18**.
- **Trade B:** felt rushed, waited for the close, finished **−$12**.
- **Trade C:** felt calm, entered early, finished **+$5**.

These notes show an early entry with two different feeling words. They also show a rushed feeling with two different actions. They do **not** show that calm caused a win or that rushing caused a loss. The result is separate from whether the written check happened.

You may find too few similar entries to say anything useful yet. That is an honest review result. Keep recording at the same point, and return to the question later. Even when a pattern appears in your journal, it is a reason to inspect your process, not proof that an emotion caused the trade result.
