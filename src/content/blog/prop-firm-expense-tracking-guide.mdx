You may remember the payout clearly. But can you list every challenge purchase, renewal, activation cost, platform fee, refund, and earlier attempt that came before it?

If those payments live across emails, bank statements, screenshots, and memory, it becomes hard to answer a simple question: **what did this prop-firm journey actually cost you?**

Prop firm expense tracking means giving every real payment or receipt its own row. Record the date, firm, account or attempt, category, USD amount, status, evidence, and one short note. Do this when the event happens instead of rebuilding the story months later.

> **Quick answer:** one payment, refund, or payout should create one row. Never replace an older row when something changes. Add the new event so the full history remains visible.

## Start with the payment, not the trading result

Suppose you buy a new challenge after an earlier attempt ends. You may feel focused on the new account and stop thinking about the earlier fee. Your expense log should not do that.

Record the new purchase as a new row. Keep the earlier row. If the firm later sends a refund, add the refund as another row instead of changing the original purchase to zero.

This gives you a timeline you can check:

1. what you paid;
2. what the payment was for;
3. which account or attempt it belonged to;
4. whether it was paid, pending, received, or refunded; and
5. where the receipt or dashboard record can be found.

The trade result belongs in your [prop firm trading journal](/blogs/prop-firm-trading-journal). The cash event belongs in the expense log. You can review them together later without mixing the two records.

## Give every event one clear category

Your exact costs depend on the firm, program, account type, promotion, and date. Do not copy a “typical fee” from another trader. Use the amount shown on your own invoice, receipt, payment record, or current firm dashboard.

Useful categories can include:

| Category | What the row records | Evidence to keep |
|---|---|---|
| Challenge purchase | A new evaluation or account purchase | Invoice, receipt, order email |
| Renewal or reset | A payment that continues or restarts an attempt | Renewal or reset confirmation |
| Activation | A separate cost charged after a stage is completed | Activation invoice or dashboard entry |
| Platform or data | A cost tied to the account or trading access | Subscription receipt and covered period |
| Refund | Money returned for an earlier payment | Refund confirmation and original row reference |
| Payout received | Money that has actually arrived | Payout record and received date |

Not every firm uses every category. If a cost does not exist in your program, do not create it. If a payment does not fit, use “other” and explain it in the note.

## Record these fields in every row

Keep the log short enough that you will actually complete it.

### Date

Use the date the money was paid or received. If a payout is only requested or pending, keep that status visible. Do not record it as received until the status changes.

### Firm and account reference

Use the same firm name every time. Add an account or attempt label such as `Challenge 02`, `Account B`, or the reference from your dashboard. Stable labels help you group related rows later.

A [funded account](/glossary/funded-account) is an account stage governed by the firm's agreement. The label does not tell you whether the account uses simulated or live-market capital, and it does not confirm that a payout has been received. Keep the account stage and the cash event in separate fields.

### Record type and category

Separate money going out from money coming in. Then choose the category that explains why it moved.

- **Expense:** money you paid.
- **Payout:** money you actually received.
- **Refund:** money returned from an earlier expense.

### Amount in USD

PropLogAI forex and prop-firm examples use dollars. Record the amount in USD. If your payment method charged another currency, keep the original statement and write the USD amount you chose to use for this log consistently.

### Status

Use a short status such as `paid`, `received`, `pending`, or `refunded`. A status tells you what happened without forcing you to rewrite the row.

### Receipt reference and note

You do not need a long explanation. Write where you can find the evidence and what makes this row different from another one.

Examples:

- `Email receipt 2026-09-25`;
- `Challenge 02 — separate from first attempt`;
- `Pending payout request — do not count as received`;
- `Refund for record EXP-014`.

## Review the log without turning it into a verdict

At the end of the month, ask factual questions:

- Are any receipts or payout records missing?
- Did one payment create more than one row by mistake?
- Are pending payouts still marked as pending?
- Can every refund be connected to the original expense?
- Are firm names and account references consistent?
- Which categories created the most recorded costs during this period?
- Do your journal notes explain what happened around repeated purchases or renewals?

Do not use one month to label yourself a good or bad trader. The log shows money that moved. Your journal shows the decisions and process around it.

## Keep expense tracking separate from ROI calculation

This guide owns the record-keeping process. It helps you create clean inputs.

The [prop firm ROI calculator guide](/blogs/prop-firm-roi-calculator) explains how to compare total recorded costs with payouts across attempts. Use that calculation only after your rows are complete. A formula cannot repair missing purchases, refunds, or pending payouts.

You may also want two views:

1. **By firm:** group every expense, refund, and payout connected to one firm.
2. **By campaign or period:** group the attempts you deliberately want to review together and state the start and end dates.

Do not quietly change the group until the result looks better. Write the scope beside the calculation.

## Common mistakes to avoid

**Remembering only the latest attempt.** Keep every earlier purchase and renewal visible.

**Replacing a purchase with a refund.** Add the refund as a new row and connect it to the original expense.

**Counting a pending payout as received.** Keep the status pending until the money arrives.

**Using vague account names.** `Second account` becomes confusing when you have several firms. Use a stable attempt or account reference.

**Copying another trader's fee list.** Your current invoice and program terms are the relevant sources for your record.

**Mixing tax records with a review log.** This template is for personal tracking. Use qualified local advice for tax or accounting decisions.

## Frequently asked questions

### What prop firm expenses should I track?

Track every amount you actually pay that you decide belongs to the prop-firm activity. Common record categories include challenge purchases, renewals or resets, activation charges, and platform or data costs. Use your own receipts because firms and programs differ.

### Should I include failed challenges?

Yes, if you paid for the attempt. The payment remains a real expense even when the challenge ends. Record the result separately in your journal.

### Should I record a payout when I request it?

You can record the request with a pending status, but do not mark it received until it arrives. This keeps requested and received amounts separate.

Before you submit it, use the [prop firm payout rules checklist](/blogs/prop-firm-payout-rules) to separate dashboard profit, request eligibility, approval, and money received.

### Should Indian traders convert everything to INR?

PropLogAI forex and prop-firm examples use USD. Keep the expense log in USD for a consistent comparison. Preserve the original payment statement when another currency was charged.

### Is this expense log enough for taxes?

No. It is a personal review record, not tax or accounting advice. Tax treatment and documentation requirements depend on your circumstances and local rules.

### How often should I review the log?

Add each row when the payment, refund, or payout happens. A short monthly check is useful for missing evidence, duplicate rows, inconsistent labels, and pending items.

The goal is simple: when you ask what your prop-firm activity cost, you should be able to answer from records instead of memory.
