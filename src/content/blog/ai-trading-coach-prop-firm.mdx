You ask an AI trading coach about your XAUUSD trades from the London session. It answers, “Three of your six logged trades were marked as early entries.” That sounds useful. **Before you change your trading plan, can you open the three records behind that sentence?**

XAUUSD means gold quoted in US dollars. In the **fictional example below**, your setup is a breakout: price moves beyond a level you marked. Your own written check says to wait for the breakout candle to close before entering. “Early” means *before that check*, not that a trade lost money.

> **Quick answer:** an [AI trading coach](https://proplogai.com/glossary/ai-trading-coach) can help you review information you recorded. It may point to a possible pattern or a useful question. The answer is only as good as the records behind it, so check those records yourself. It cannot choose your next trade.

## Start with the records you actually have

Your [trading journal](https://proplogai.com/glossary/trading-journal) is where you record your plan, what you did and what happened. For this example, you logged six XAUUSD London-session breakout trades. Each has a date, your setup label, a note about the candle-close check and an “early entry?” label.

Ask one narrow question: **“Which of these six entries did I mark as entering before my candle-close check?”** You know which account, period and setup you mean. The AI has a smaller job than “tell me what is wrong with my trading.”

You also know the limit: if you forgot to log a trade, it is not in this six-trade sample. If you did not record how you felt, the coach cannot safely tell you what emotion caused a decision. The journal is a record you made, not a live broker statement.

## Treat the AI answer as a pointer

Imagine the coach says, **“Three of the six records are marked early.”** That is a possible summary of the labels you entered. It does not show whether each label is right. It does not say the early entries caused a loss or that the next breakout will work.

This is [pattern recognition](https://proplogai.com/glossary/pattern-recognition) in a journal: looking for something that appears more than once in a defined set of *past records*. It is different from finding a chart pattern to predict price. If the answer sounds certain, that is still not a reason to skip the record check.

Open those three entries. Look at the saved note, label and any screenshot you recorded. Ask whether your written candle-close condition had actually happened when you entered. If the note lacks the timing or the screenshot cannot show it, mark that entry **unclear** rather than asking AI to fill in the missing fact.

## Correct the record before you decide what it means

In this **fictional sample**, you find that one “early” label was entered by mistake. Your original note and saved chart show that your planned candle-close check had happened. You correct that label. The revised journal now has **two of six** entries marked early, not three of six.

That correction matters. An AI answer based on the old label can sound precise and still be wrong about what you did. Even the corrected count is only about these six logged trades. It does not prove *why* you entered early, whether the behaviour will repeat, or whether a different rule would improve your results. A green or red P&L figure does not settle whether you followed your plan.

A [trade review](https://proplogai.com/glossary/trade-review) is the later check of your planned action against what you actually recorded. Your next question might be: **“Were the two early entries in the same session or setup, and what do my notes say?”** You can inspect those entries before deciding whether anything in your own plan needs to be clearer.

## Know what a journal coach cannot decide

A review question looks back: “Which recorded entries did not match my written check?” A trading signal looks forward: “Should I buy or sell now?” Those are different jobs. A journal coach should not turn a past observation into an entry, exit, price target or promise that you will pass a challenge.

It also cannot confirm the current rules of your prop-firm account from your journal alone. Firms, programs, stages and account dates can differ. Read the official terms and your account dashboard for an actual limit or breach question; the [prop-firm rules guide](/blogs/prop-firm-rules-guide) explains how to organise that check.

Before trusting a coach answer, ask:

1. Which account, dates and recorded trades did it use?
2. Can I open the entries behind the statement?
3. Are the setup, action and result labels correct? What is missing?
4. Is this a description of old records, or has it become advice about my next trade?
