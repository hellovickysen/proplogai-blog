You took an XAUUSD breakout trade during the London session. You waited for the candle to close beyond your marked level, just as your plan said. The trade lost $50.

Now you feel the urge to enter again. The next candle is still open, but you think, “If I wait, I might miss it.” You enter before your confirmation appears. This second trade wins $80.

Which trade showed better discipline? **The first one.** The loss hurts, but you followed your plan. The $80 win does not erase the fact that you skipped your own entry check.

That is the kind of question an [AI trading coach](/glossary/ai-trading-coach) can help you review. It looks at decisions you recorded in your journal. It should not choose your next trade for you.

## Start with the plan you wrote before the trade

A [trading plan](/glossary/trading-plan) is your written guide for when you will take a trade and how you will manage it. For this **fictional** example, your XAUUSD London breakout plan has one entry check: *wait for the candle to close beyond the marked level before entering.* A candle that is still moving has not passed that check.

Write that condition down before you know the result. If you write it only after the trade, it is easy to make the plan fit what happened.

## Record the decision and the result separately

Your [trading journal](/glossary/trading-journal) is a record of the trades you actually took. Record the setup condition, whether you followed it, the result in USD, and a short note about why you entered. A journal is useful for comparing a planned trade with what you did; [IG's journal guide](https://www.ig.com/uk/learn-to-trade/ig-academy/tools-for-traders/important-metrics?source=dailyfx) also recommends keeping trade details for later review.

Mark [setup compliance](/glossary/setup-compliance)—whether your entry met the conditions you wrote—as **Yes**, **No**, or **Not recorded**. “Not recorded” means you cannot fairly score that decision yet. It is not the same as “No.”

The result tells you what happened to the money. It does not tell you whether you followed your plan. This is why a winning trade can still need a discipline review.

If you forgot to record the entry check, mark it **Not recorded**. Look at the trade notes or chart later if you have them. Do not invent an answer to make the journal look complete.

## Ask AI a question about your own records

Once you have recorded the entries, you can ask a narrow question such as:

> “From the trades I logged, when did I mark that I entered before my own breakout confirmation?”

An AI review may help you find and summarize those recorded entries. Then **open the underlying trades yourself**. Check whether you logged the setup the same way each time and whether any fields are missing. A summary is a starting point, not proof of why you acted.

For example, you might notice several early entries after a loss. That may be worth checking in your notes, but it does not prove the loss *caused* the next decision. The [NIST statistics handbook](https://www.itl.nist.gov/div898/handbook/ppc/section1/ppc136.htm) explains why an association alone does not establish cause.

## Keep the next review small

At the end of a week, you can ask yourself three things:

1. Which trades have a clear **Yes**, **No**, or **Not recorded** entry check?
2. Did I judge a trade by whether I followed my plan, separately from whether it won?
3. Is there one pattern I want to check against the original notes next week?

You do not need to set a universal trade count or invent a new rule from one example. If your records are incomplete, improve the recording first. If you suspect a pattern, watch it over more trades before deciding what it means.
