Your recent XAUUSD trades felt good. You have a paid [prop firm challenge](/glossary/prop-firm-challenge) page open and your finger is near **Buy**. It is easy to think, “Maybe I am ready now.”

Pause for two checks. **Do you know the current rules for this exact program? Do your recorded trades show how you followed your own plan?** A good week cannot answer either question for you.

## Copy this before-you-pay worksheet

You can paste this into your notes. Leave an answer as **“not checked”** or **“not recorded”** if that is the truth. This sheet is a way to find missing information, not a readiness score.

## First, check the exact program rules

Open the firm's current official terms. Write down the program, account size and stage you might buy. A firm's other program may work differently. For example, [FTMO's published Trading Objectives](https://ftmo.com/en/trading-objectives/) show different conditions for its 1-Step and 2-Step programs. That page was checked on 11 October 2026; check it again if you are considering either program.

Find the profit target and the [daily drawdown limit](/glossary/daily-drawdown-limit)—the most the account is allowed to fall under that program's daily method. Also check the [overall drawdown limit](/glossary/overall-drawdown-limit), or the account-wide loss boundary. Ask whether open trades and costs count, how each limit moves, and **when the firm's day resets in its stated time zone**. Your local clock in India may show a different date. Check any trading-day, news, holding, consistency or instrument restriction that applies, plus the fee and account terms.

Do not guess the consequence of a missed condition. One rule may end an attempt; another may delay eligibility. The official terms for the exact program decide. Our [prop firm rules guide](/blogs/prop-firm-rules-guide) helps you turn those terms into a one-page rule sheet. If your program has a consistency condition, use the [consistency rule guide](/blogs/prop-firm-consistency-calculator) to check its exact calculation and consequence.

## Then, check your plan against your records

Your [trading plan](/glossary/trading-plan) is the rule you wrote for when to enter, when the idea is wrong, and when to leave. Use your own setup; you do not have to trade a particular chart pattern. Write the session and your planned [risk per trade](/glossary/risk-per-trade), meaning the dollar amount you planned to lose if the trade reached your stop. A reliable calculator is fine. Check its inputs and compare the result with the firm's loss limits before trading.

Now open your [trading journal](/blogs/prop-firm-trading-journal). Look at trades from a comparable setup and session. For each one, compare the decision you recorded with the rule you had **at the time**. A profitable trade might still have broken your plan. A losing trade might have followed it. If you did not record the original stop or an entry reason, write “not recorded.” A stop value entered later does not prove that the stop was never moved.

## A fictional XAUUSD example

Imagine your written setup is an XAUUSD breakout during the London session. Your own rule says to enter only after a candle closes beyond your marked level. These are four **fictional** trades, with USD results before costs the journal did not record. This is an example of checking decisions, not a suggested setup.

| Trade | Session and setup | Followed this trader's candle-close rule? | Recorded USD result |
|---|---|---|---:|
| 1 | London XAUUSD breakout | Yes | +$80 |
| 2 | London XAUUSD breakout | Yes | -$50 |
| 3 | London XAUUSD breakout | No — entered before the close | +$45 |
| 4 | London XAUUSD breakout | Yes | -$60 |

On a phone, swipe the table sideways to read each column.

The recorded total is **+$15**: $80 − $50 + $45 − $60. That is **before unrecorded costs**. Three entries followed the fictional rule. Trade 3 made **+$45** even though the trader entered early. The dollar total and the plan check tell different stories.

## What do you do with the answers?

If the rules are clear and you have records to review, read the offer's current terms and make your own decision. If a rule is unknown, get the answer from the firm before you pay. If your behaviour was not recorded, practise your own plan and start recording it. Do not turn a few trades or checked boxes into a promise of passing.

PropLogAI can help you keep manual trade details, notes, rule-following tags and screenshots together, then view dashboard measures and a P&amp;L calendar. Those records can help you ask better questions. They do not certify readiness or automatically prove that an original stop was respected.

If the program's terms change, check them again before making a purchase. **“Not yet” is an honest answer when a rule or part of your own record is still missing.**
