You open your trading calendar and Wednesday is red: **−$110**. You may feel you traded badly. But the colour tells you only the result you recorded. It cannot tell you whether you followed your plan.

**Look up that day's trades in your journal. Compare each entry with the check you wrote before trading. Then write down what the records actually show.** That is a useful [trade review](/glossary/trade-review); a red square by itself is not one.

## What a P&L calendar shows

A P&L calendar places your **logged profit or loss** on each trading day. Depending on what you recorded, you may also see the number of trades. It helps you choose a day to examine. It does not know why you entered, whether you followed your rule, or why the market moved.

If a trade or fee is missing from your journal, the calendar total may be incomplete. Keep that gap visible. Also, do not treat a calendar colour as your prop firm's decision about a breach. For your official limits and account status, check your own firm's current terms and dashboard for your program and stage.

## Start with a fictional XAUUSD week

Imagine you trade an **XAUUSD London-session breakout**. XAUUSD is gold priced in US dollars. A breakout is a move beyond a level you marked on the chart. Your written entry check for this example is: **wait for a candle to close beyond the marked level**. A candle is one price bar for the time period you chose. This is a way to review your own rule, not an entry instruction.

All five days below are fictional. These are amounts entered in a journal, **before fees that were not recorded**. “Followed” means the trade record shows the trader waited for the required close. On a phone, swipe the table sideways.

| Day | Logged P&L | Trades | Followed the written check |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Monday | −$40 | 2 | 2 of 2 |
| Tuesday | +$75 | 3 | 2 of 3 |
| Wednesday | −$110 | 4 | 2 of 4 |
| Thursday | $0 | 0 | No trades |
| Friday | +$35 | 2 | 2 of 2 |
| **Week** | **−$40** | **11** | **8 of 11** |

The sums are **−$40 + $75 − $110 + $0 + $35 = −$40**, **2 + 3 + 4 + 0 + 2 = 11 trades**, and **2 + 2 + 2 + 0 + 2 = 8** that followed this one check. The −$40 is recorded P&L, not a verified net result: fees were not captured.

## Open Wednesday's four trades

Now find Wednesday's four trades in the journal. The calendar shows **−$110**, but you need the entry notes or screenshots to compare those trades with the written candle-close check. Here is what the fictional records say:

| Wednesday trade | Logged result | What the entry record shows | Followed the check? |
| --- | ---: | --- | --- |
| 1 | −$30 | Entered after the candle closed beyond the level | Yes |
| 2 | −$20 | Entered before that candle closed | No |
| 3 | −$35 | Entered after the candle closed beyond the level | Yes |
| 4 | −$25 | Entered before that candle closed | No |

Those four results add to **−$110**. Two entries met the trader's check and two did not. The records show an action; they do **not** prove that entering early caused either loss. If a real record does not say when the entry happened, mark it **“not recorded”** rather than deciding from the dollar amount.

You might wonder whether Wednesday was [revenge trading](/glossary/revenge-trading)—taking another trade to try to win back a loss. The calendar cannot answer that. Check the order of trades and any note about the reason for each entry. Even then, describe the recorded action before naming a motive. Here the entry timing is recorded; the trader's reason for trades 2 and 4 is **not recorded**.

Tuesday gives the opposite warning. It finished **+$75**, yet one of its three entries was made before the planned close. A green day does not turn that early entry into a plan-following one.

## Write one short note about the day

Your daily note can be brief. Keep the result, the action, missing facts, and your next question in separate lines:

<div class="monthly-review-template monthly-review-template--filled" aria-label="Completed fictional Wednesday P and L calendar review note">
  <p><strong>Day and setup:</strong> Wednesday, fictional XAUUSD London breakout.</p>
  <p><strong>Logged result:</strong> −$110 across four trades; fees not recorded.</p>
  <p><strong>My written check:</strong> Wait for a candle close beyond the marked level.</p>
  <p><strong>What the records show:</strong> Trades 1 and 3 followed that check. Trades 2 and 4 entered before the close.</p>
  <p><strong>What is missing:</strong> The reasons for trades 2 and 4, plus fees. No firm-rule verdict is recorded.</p>
  <p><strong>Next question:</strong> On the next comparable breakout, did I wait for the close in my written plan?</p>
</div>

If your plan already has a stop rule, check the exact version you used that day. Do not invent a rule after seeing a loss. If you need a consistent place for entry reasons, screenshots and fees, see [which trading journal fields to record](/blogs/trading-journal-fields).

## What to do when you look at the whole week

The week finished at **−$40 recorded P&L**, with **8 of 11** trades meeting this one written check. That is enough to point you back to Tuesday's early entry and Wednesday's two early entries. It is not enough to say the setup is profitable or unprofitable, or to predict your next week.

For a wider view, put these daily notes into a [weekly trading review](/blogs/weekly-trading-review-template), then compare weeks in a [monthly trading review](/blogs/monthly-trading-review-template). The [prop-firm trading journal guide](/blogs/prop-firm-trading-journal) explains the full record-to-review workflow. If you later want to interpret win rate, drawdown or profit factor, use the [trading performance metrics guide](/blogs/trading-performance-metrics); the calendar colour does not explain those numbers either.

**Next time you see a red day, check its trades before judging it.** And do the same with a green day. The useful question is what you actually recorded about your own plan, not what the colour makes you feel.
