You finish the week with **$800 total net profit**. Your best day was **$400** after an XAUUSD liquidity-sweep setup during the London session.

You may feel that the week was steady because four other days were smaller. But if your program uses a best-day consistency rule, the calculation is:

```text
$400 best day ÷ $800 total net profit × 100 = 50%
```

That number is only useful when you compare it with the exact rule for your program.

> **Quick answer:** when a firm uses the best-day formula, consistency percentage = best profit day ÷ total net profit × 100. Copy the limit, calculation window, daily cut-off, and consequence from the firm's current rules before using the result.

## Use the consistency rule calculator

Open the [existing PropLogAI consistency rule calculator](https://proplogai.com/tools/consistency-calculator) and enter:

1. your total net profit for the firm's defined period;
2. your single best profit day in that same period; and
3. the exact percentage limit stated by your firm.

The calculator shows the ratio created by those three numbers. It does **not** decide whether your account passed, whether a payout can be requested, or whether you should take another trade.

Use it only when your program uses this formula:

**Consistency percentage = best profit day ÷ total net profit × 100**

If your firm uses a different numerator, denominator, time window, or calculation method, this calculator is not the correct tool for that rule.

## A five-day XAUUSD example

Imagine these are your fictional daily net results. The setup names help you identify the day in your journal; they do not change the arithmetic.

| Day | Session and setup | Daily net result |
|---|---|---:|
| Monday | London liquidity sweep | +$400 |
| Tuesday | London breakout | +$250 |
| Wednesday | No trade | $0 |
| Thursday | New York breakout | −$150 |
| Friday | London liquidity sweep | +$300 |
| **Total** |  | **+$800** |

The best day is Monday at **$400**. The total net profit is **$800**.

```text
Consistency percentage = $400 ÷ $800 × 100
                       = 50%
```

If your official limit were 40%, the calculated 50% would be above the limit you entered. That sentence describes the arithmetic only. Your firm's dashboard and current rules decide what happens next.

## Why a losing day can increase the percentage

Before Thursday's **−$150** result, total profit was $950. The same $400 best day represented about 42.1% of that total.

After the loss, total profit fell to $800 while the best day stayed at $400. The ratio increased to 50%.

This is why you should include losing days when the official formula uses **total net profit**. Removing them would create a different number.

## The calculator result is not a trade target

Suppose your best day is $400 and the limit you copied is 40%. The arithmetic says the denominator would need to be at least $1,000 for $400 to represent 40%:

```text
Minimum denominator for the entered ratio = $400 ÷ 0.40
                                           = $1,000
```

If your current total is $800, the mathematical gap is $200. That is **not** an instruction to make another $200, increase size, stay in the market, or force an extra trading day.

You may feel urged to “fix” the number quickly. Pause there. A calculator cannot see your setup quality, your [daily drawdown limit](/glossary/daily-drawdown-limit), the market conditions, or the rest of your agreement. Follow the trading plan you already wrote and use the firm's official status as the final check.

## Why the program name matters

A [consistency rule](/glossary/consistency-rule) does not have one universal percentage or consequence.

Topstep's official consistency page was rechecked on 29 September 2026. It shows two different examples inside one firm:

- **Trading Combine:** a 55% Consistency Target using best-day profit against total profit. When the target is exceeded, the required profit target can increase.
- **Express Funded Account Consistency path:** a 40% objective using largest single-day net profit against total net profit. A result above the limit does not satisfy that payout-path requirement, and the calculation resets after a payout request under the published conditions.

These are named examples, not default settings for every prop firm. They also show why a calculator should not label an account “payout ready” from three inputs.

Before calculating, confirm all of these:

- firm and exact program;
- evaluation or funded stage;
- best **day**, best trade, or another numerator;
- total net profit or another denominator;
- whether losing days reduce the denominator;
- daily cut-off and timezone;
- calculation window and reset rule; and
- the actual consequence when the limit is exceeded.

## Consistency rule, profit target, and risk limits

These rules answer different questions.

| Rule | What it checks | What the number cannot tell you |
|---|---|---|
| Consistency rule | How concentrated profit is under the firm's formula | Whether your next trade is valid |
| [Profit target](/glossary/profit-target) | Whether the required net-profit objective was reached | Whether every other rule was met |
| Daily drawdown limit | The permitted account value during the defined day | Whether the overall limit is safe |
| [Overall drawdown limit](/glossary/overall-drawdown-limit) | The account-wide floor under a static or trailing method | Whether the daily rule is safe |

## Common mistakes

- Entering gross winning-day profit instead of the firm's defined daily net result.
- Removing losing days from total net profit.
- Copying a threshold from another firm, program, or payout path.
- Mixing dates from two calculation windows.
- Treating a calculator result as official payout approval.
- Turning the displayed gap into a target for the next trade.
- Assuming the rule proves that a strategy will perform consistently in the future.

## Frequently asked questions

### What is a prop firm consistency rule?

It is a program-specific calculation used to measure how concentrated a result is. A common form compares the best profit day with total net profit, but the percentage, period, consequence, and account stage come from the exact program rules.

### How do I calculate a 40% consistency rule?

Only use 40% when your current program states that limit and uses the best-day formula. Divide the best profit day by total net profit and multiply by 100. A $400 best day divided by $1,000 total net profit equals 40%.

### Do losing days affect the calculation?

They do when the firm's denominator is total net profit. A losing day reduces that total, so the same best day can become a larger percentage. Confirm the definition used by your program.

### Does a result under the entered limit mean I can request a payout?

No. It means the entered numbers produced a ratio at or below the limit you entered. Payout eligibility can depend on other rules, account status, timing, and the firm's official review.

### Should I keep trading to reduce the percentage?

The calculator cannot answer that. Do not use the displayed gap as a reason to force a trade. Check the official rule and follow your existing trading plan.

### Is the best day the same as the best trade?

No. A best day is the net result of the firm's defined trading day. A best trade is one position or transaction. Use this calculator only when the official rule uses a best-day numerator.
