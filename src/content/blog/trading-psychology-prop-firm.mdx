Imagine your XAUUSD trade closes at **−$50** during the London session. You feel the urge to get that money back. A new move starts, and you want to enter before your usual check is complete.

XAUUSD means gold priced in US dollars. In this example, your setup is a **breakout**: price moves beyond a level you marked before the session. Your written plan says to wait for the breakout candle to close before you decide whether to enter.

The feeling is not the mistake. The useful question is: **did that feeling change your next decision?**

> **Quick answer:** trading psychology is about how your feelings and expectations may affect what you notice, choose and do. Compare your action with the [trading plan](https://proplogai.com/glossary/trading-plan) you wrote before the pressure appeared. A win does not prove you followed it, and a loss does not prove you broke it.

## Start with the plan you made before the urge

You cannot tell what changed if you only look at the trade after it closes. First, write down the conditions you meant to follow.

Here is one **fictional** XAUUSD London-session plan:

- **Setup:** a breakout beyond a level marked before London opens.
- **Confirmation:** wait for the breakout candle to close. A candle is one price bar for your chosen time period.
- **Size:** use the same position-sizing method already written in your own plan.
- **Stop rule:** if you feel pushed to recover the last loss, check the full setup again before any new decision. This is the fictional trader's rule, not a rule for every trader or firm.

The plan does not tell you where gold will go. It gives you a fair way to check whether the next action came from your process or from a sudden urge.

## Notice what the last result makes you want to do

Your first trade loses $50. That number is a fictional **net result**, not a suggested risk amount. You may think, “If I take the next move now, I can make it back.”

That thought can be useful information. It tells you **why** another entry suddenly feels urgent. It does not tell you whether a new setup is valid. Before you decide, ask what changed:

1. Has a fresh breakout candle actually closed, as your plan requires?
2. Is the setup still the one you marked before the session?
3. Would you use the same size if the previous trade had been a win?
4. Are you considering this trade because the setup is present, or mainly because you want to recover $50?

You might answer these questions and find that the new trade still meets your plan. Another trade after a loss is **not automatically** [revenge trading](https://proplogai.com/glossary/revenge-trading). Revenge trading means the urge to recover the loss starts to drive the next decision. The [post-loss guide](/blogs/revenge-trading-prop-firm) takes that moment further.

Or you might find that you are about to enter before the candle closes. Now you can name the change: **your normal confirmation is missing**. That is clearer than calling yourself “undisciplined.”

## Review the decision separately from the result

Imagine you enter early and the second trade wins **$35 net**. That result does not change the fact that you skipped your own candle-close condition. If the trade loses instead, the skipped condition is the same. **Review the action first, then record the result.**

Use a short journal note with five separate lines:

1. **Feeling:** “Frustrated after the first loss.”
2. **Urge:** “Wanted to recover $50 straight away.”
3. **Action:** “Entered before the breakout candle closed.”
4. **Plan check:** “Did not follow my written confirmation rule.”
5. **Net result:** “+$35 after costs.”

The opposite can happen too. You may wait for your planned check, take a valid setup and lose. That loss alone does not show a psychology problem. Your note should say what you did, not judge your character from the profit or loss.

This simple record is [emotion tracking](https://proplogai.com/glossary/emotion-tracking): writing down a feeling beside the decision it may have influenced. The detailed [emotion-tracking guide](/blogs/tracking-trading-emotions) shows how to keep these notes useful without turning your journal into a diary.

## Look for your pattern, not a label that fits everyone

The same feeling can lead to different actions on different days. A missed move might bring [FOMO](https://proplogai.com/glossary/fomo), meaning fear of missing out, and tempt you to enter late. A win might bring [overconfidence](https://proplogai.com/glossary/overconfidence), where the last result makes a weaker setup look safe. [Loss aversion](https://proplogai.com/glossary/loss-aversion) is when avoiding a loss weighs heavily in a choice, perhaps leading you to change an exit you had already planned.

These are **possible** explanations, not diagnoses. Do not decide which one applies from a single red or green trade. Write down what you felt, what you did and what your plan required. After several sessions, see whether the same trigger appears before the same kind of plan change. A small pattern is a reason to look closer, not proof that a feeling caused a loss.

If you want to focus on one decision, see [how emotions affect trading decisions](/blogs/how-emotions-affect-trading-decisions). If your problem is repeated extra entries, the [overtrading guide](/blogs/overtrading-prop-firm-challenges) checks the behaviour against your plan rather than a fixed trade count.

## Prop-firm pressure changes the context, not your method of review

An account rule can make a normal loss feel heavier. But there is no single deadline, loss formula or consistency rule that applies to every prop-firm account. Rules can differ by firm, program, stage and account date. Check the terms and dashboard for **your exact account** before relying on a limit. [FTMO's published objectives](https://ftmo.com/en/trading-objectives/) show how terms can differ even within one firm; they are not rules for an account at another firm.

Your journal can tell you whether you followed **your own plan**. It cannot give an official breach or payout verdict. The [prop-firm rules guide](/blogs/prop-firm-rules-guide) explains how to record the current rule source without confusing your note with the firm's decision.

## Choose one thing to make easier next session

You do not need a promise to “never trade emotionally.” Try one check that fits **your** written plan. For example: put your candle-close condition beside the chart, then record whether it was met before you entered. If your own stop rule says to step away after a trigger, make that rule clear before the session starts. Do not borrow somebody else's fixed trade limit and assume it fits your account.

PropLogAI lets you manually record trade details, notes, emotions and whether you followed your own rules. Those entries can support a review of your decisions. They are not trade signals, automatic protection from rule breaks or a psychological diagnosis.

For a repeatable before-and-after routine, use the [trading discipline guide](/blogs/trading-discipline-prop-firm). The point of this page is smaller: when you feel an urge, **check what changed from the plan**. That gives you something specific to learn from, whatever the next trade earns or loses.
