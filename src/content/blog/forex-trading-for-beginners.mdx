You see an XAUUSD breakout during the London session. It looks exciting, and you feel ready to place an order. But can you explain what XAUUSD is, what you could lose, and which rules apply to your account?

You do not need to learn every trading word today. Start with those three questions. This is a **fictional learning scene**, not a trade to copy.

## First, know what you are trading

In forex, you trade one currency against another. **EURUSD** compares the euro with the US dollar. If the EURUSD price changes, the value of one euro in dollars has changed.

**XAUUSD is different.** It is gold quoted in US dollars, not a pair of two currencies. Some traders follow both currency pairs and gold, but each instrument has its own price movement and contract details. Do not assume that a size or price move means the same dollar result on both.

Your **trade size** is how much of the instrument you buy or sell. A **lot** is one way a trading platform describes that size; the units behind a lot depend on the instrument and broker. The same price move can mean a different gain or loss when your size changes. Before you trade, check the symbol details on your own platform.

You may also see **spread**, the gap between the buy and sell prices, and **commission**, a fee your broker may charge. They affect your result. **Leverage** lets a smaller amount of account money control a larger position. That larger exposure can magnify a loss as well as a gain. You still need to understand the dollar amount at risk, not just the amount your platform asks you to put up.

## Next, write the plan before the trade

Return to that fictional London-session XAUUSD breakout. Suppose your own written setup says a breakout is worth considering only after you have checked its conditions. Before any order, your note might look like this:

<div class="weekly-review-template" aria-label="Fictional XAUUSD pre-trade learning note">
  <p><strong>Instrument:</strong> XAUUSD — gold quoted in US dollars</p>
  <p><strong>Setup and session:</strong> My written breakout setup, London session</p>
  <p><strong>Planned stop:</strong> Write the price and why it belongs there before choosing trade size</p>
  <p><strong>Estimated loss:</strong> Fictional example: $50, after checking the symbol's value and expected costs</p>
  <p><strong>Account rule:</strong> Check the exact firm's daily limit and what already happened today</p>
</div>

The planned [stop-loss](https://proplogai.com/glossary/stop-loss) is your intended exit if the trade goes against you. It does **not** guarantee that you can lose only $50. The actual exit price and costs can change the final amount. The [position size](https://proplogai.com/glossary/position-sizing) must be calculated using the instrument's units, the distance to that stop, your account currency and expected costs. Use the [position-size guide](/blogs/how-to-calculate-position-size-forex) for the full calculation; the $50 here is only a fictional planned amount, not a suggested amount for your account.

You may also compare the amount you plan to lose with the amount you hope to make. That is a [risk-to-reward ratio](https://proplogai.com/glossary/risk-reward-ratio). It helps you describe the plan, but it cannot tell you whether this trade will win. If you have not defined the setup and stop, a neat-looking ratio will not fix the missing plan.

## A prop-firm challenge adds another set of rules

A [prop-firm challenge](https://proplogai.com/glossary/prop-firm-challenge) is an evaluation with rules set by that firm and program. Those rules may cover a profit target, a [daily drawdown limit](https://proplogai.com/glossary/daily-drawdown-limit), an overall loss limit, or other conditions. **The exact formula, reset time, stage and consequence matter.** Do not copy another trader's rule sheet and assume it applies to you.

For example, a $50 *planned* loss from the note tells you nothing about how much room you have left today unless you check today's closed result, open trades, costs and the firm's current way of measuring its limit. Your clock in India may not match the firm's reset clock. Read the official terms for the exact account before treating any number as safe. The [prop-firm rules guide](/blogs/prop-firm-rules-guide) shows how to turn those terms into a usable rule sheet.

## A simple order for learning

You do not have to buy a challenge to practise these steps:

1. **Know the instrument.** Can you explain what EURUSD or XAUUSD represents and where to check its contract details?
2. **Practise one setup.** Write what a breakout or another setup means in your own [trading plan](https://proplogai.com/glossary/trading-plan). Use a demo account to see how orders and costs work; a demo result is not proof that a later challenge will go the same way.
3. **Plan the risk.** Write your stop and estimated dollar loss, then calculate size. The [forex risk-management guide](/blogs/prop-firm-risk-management) connects one trade with other open risk and today's result.
4. **Read the firm's rules.** Check the exact firm, program, stage, daily reset and consequence on its current official page.
5. **Journal and review.** Record what you planned and what actually happened, including setup, size, costs and whether you followed your own plan. A [trading journal template](/blogs/trading-journal-template) gives you a place to start.
6. **Check readiness before paying.** Use the [challenge-readiness checklist](/blogs/prop-firm-challenge-readiness) to examine your practice records and the account terms. It is a decision aid, not a pass prediction.

If you cannot yet explain your stop, estimated USD loss or your firm's loss calculation, keep learning and testing the plan. You can return to those questions without rushing into an order or paying for an evaluation.
